# YouTube growth playbook (packaging first)

Use for any new video or episode idea, title, thumbnail or hook, and for a retention review
of a script before it's voiced. tacdel-video builds the video; this skill decides what's worth
building and how it's packaged. Keep replies short, plain and in simple words.

## Core ideas

1. **Packaging first.** Idea, then title, then thumbnail, before any work. If no title and
   thumbnail would make a stranger click, change or drop the idea.
2. **Two numbers decide everything:** click-through rate (CTR) and watch time (retention).
   Every choice should serve one of them.
3. **Simple.** A kid can say the premise in one sentence.
4. **Stakes and contrast.** Winners and losers; extremes (cheap vs expensive, 1 vs 100,
   last one standing).
5. **Honest hype.** Strong, plain words that are literally true. The first minute shows what
   the title promised. A title that oversells costs more retention than a plain one.
6. **Characters.** At most three main ones, each with one colour, a personality and a rivalry.
7. **Escalation.** Every 1-2 minutes a new reason to keep watching: the stakes rise, a new
   question, a twist, an elimination. Each chapter opens with its own mini-hook, written like
   a mini title.
8. **Learn from every video.** Read where the retention graph drops, test thumbnails, and
   change one thing at a time.

## Brainstorm procedure (show every step to the owner)

1. List the assets that already exist (simulations, engines, agents, footage) and the formats
   that work: elimination, 1 vs many, cheap vs expensive, underdog vs champion, builder vs
   breaker, escalating challenge, watch-it-learn.
2. **Round 1, ideas:** write 20 different ideas fast, each one written as a title (60
   characters or fewer), without judging.
3. **Quick cut, then score.** First drop any idea that can't be delivered honestly or costs
   far too much to make. Flag ideas too simple to carry a whole video and say how to make
   them bigger (more engineering, more visual wow, a rising challenge). Score the rest 1-5
   in one table: **Click** (would a stranger click), **Simple** (kid
   test), **Honest** (can the experiment truly deliver it), **Escalation** (rising stakes
   built in), **Cost** (5 = cheap with what exists), **Series** (repeatable). Say the scores
   are judgment, not data.
4. For the top 3: a thumbnail concept (one subject, one tension, 3 words or fewer, readable at
   phone size), the first 30 seconds, escalation beats every 1-2 minutes, and what could make
   it flop.
5. Pick one. For experiments, keep the title a question until the results are in, then
   sharpen it to the real result. Never shape results to fit a title.
6. **Round 2, titles:** for the picked idea, write 20 titles for that one idea, score them
   on Click, Simple and Honest, and pair the top 3 with a thumbnail concept each. Run the
   thumbnail lineup test on them.

## Testing a title and thumbnail before the video

- **Pilot Short:** film the cheapest real slice of the experiment (one round, one
  competitor) and post it as a Short; it doubles as the pilot run. If it pulls views, the
  topic has pull. Never show a result that wasn't made; with nothing built yet, only a
  question-style Short is allowed.
- **Lineup test:** run the owner's thumbnail lineup tool if it exists (real YouTube search
  results for the topic, the thumbnail and title inserted among them, shown at phone size
  plus a tiny squint version), or drop the thumbnail into a screenshot by hand. Does it
  stand out?
- Ask people outside tech which they'd click (an X poll, friends).
- After upload, run 2-3 thumbnails in YouTube Test & Compare; real impressions decide.
- On a small channel early numbers are noisy: trust patterns across videos over one result.

## Script retention check (before any voice)

- Do the first 15 seconds deliver the title's promise?
- Where is the first new question after the hook?
- Is there a stakes rise every 1-2 minutes, and does each chapter open with a mini-hook?
- Is there any 30-second stretch with no new picture or question?
- Does it end on the payoff or a question about this video, never a tease of another video?
